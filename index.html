MODEL NOTESTRANSPARENT BY DESIGN

Simple model.
Clear assumptions.

Monthwise is a planning aid, not a prediction of what will happen. It uses the dates and amounts you enter to show one possible path through a 30-day month.

A

Event-by-event balance

Starting cash plus each dated inflow, minus each dated outflow. Events on the same day are combined before the day’s closing balance is shown.

B

Timing, not certainty

Income dates and amounts can change. The delay scenario moves the first listed income payment later. A payment pushed past day 30 is excluded from the projection. These scenarios do not model probabilities.

C

Know what’s outside

The Rescue Planner tests one marked expense at a time, from its entered day through its latest allowed day. It does not combine changes. No interest, overdrafts, taxes, or automatic recurring bills are modelled.

Why track timing? See the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s cash-flow budgeting guide. The example figures on this page are fictional.

How to use Monthwise

Enter a starting balance, then add each expected payment and expense with the day you expect it to happen. Use the day money reaches or leaves your account—not the day it is earned or billed.

The chart starts with your opening cash and shows each day’s closing balance. Your safety buffer is a reference line. Mark only genuinely movable costs as flexible, then set the latest day they could be paid. The Rescue Planner checks one move at a time and does not model payment order within a day.

Nothing is uploaded. Your entries are kept in this browser only so the page can remember your example on this device. Use “Reset example” to clear them.
